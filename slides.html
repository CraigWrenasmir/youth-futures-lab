The challenge brief

You are consultants
to the council

Your job today: choose one part of city life and design a new idea, improvement, service, space, campaign, or invention that would make Newcastle both smarter and kinder for young people.

The two words that matter

Smart is not enough.
Kind is not enough.

Smart

Good systems, transport, information, design, decisions. A city that works well.

Kind

Safe, welcoming, creative, inclusive, affordable, accessible, human. A city that cares about people.

A smart city is not just a city with better technology. A smart city should also be kinder, fairer, safer, more creative, and more human.

Problem discovery

What's really
going on?

Solo sprint — 3 minutes. Everyone writes every problem they can see in your focus area. Aim for 3–10. Don't filter, don't discuss.
Go around. Read your lists out loud, one person at a time.
Find the overlap. What did more than one of you write? That shared problem is probably your problem.
Capture it on the team worksheet — including: what would someone only understand about this if they'd lived it?

Problem only for now — no solutions yet. If ideas arrive early, park them for the next step.

Mini pitch lesson · open strong, close strong

First line, last line —
make them count

Open

A tiny true story: "Last Tuesday at the Interchange…" · A number that stings: "The last bus home leaves at 5:40." · A question: "Where are you allowed to just… be?" — Never open with "Hi, we're Team 5 and today we're going to talk about…"

Close

An appeal: "Imagine if every teenager…" · A call to action: "This could start next term." · A callback to your opening. Land your strongest sentence — then stop. No "…yeah, so that's basically it."

Two minutes. Share the talking. Your visual supports you — it isn't your script.

Mini pitch lesson · your body is part of the pitch

Say it like you
mean it

Plant your feet. Pick your spot and own it — no wandering, no swaying.
Eyes up. Talk to the people at the back, not your poster or the floor.
Let your hands talk. Natural gestures beat pockets and folded arms.
Slower and louder than feels normal. Then pause after your biggest line — let it land.
Nervous? Good — that's fuel. One breath before you start. Everyone in this room is on your side.

These are options, not tests. Use notes, sit or stand, look at a friendly face or just above people's heads, share the talking however works for your team. We're listening for clarity and conviction — not judging eye contact.
